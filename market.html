NHS value

The cost of finding heart failure too late.

Heart failure consumes around 2% of the entire NHS budget and 5% of all emergency admissions. Most of that bill is paid for late — at the latest and most expensive possible point of detection.

£2bn

annual NHS cost of heart failure care, most of it via emergency attendance, prolonged stays and readmissions.

£3,690

average cost of an emergency heart failure admission.

£738–1,107

genuinely cash-releasing per avoided admission — the marginal 20–30% the NHS itself uses in costing guidance.

862,470

heart failure bed days in 2018/19. Median stay is 8 days, roughly double the average across all diagnoses.

We quote both figures deliberately. NHS reference costs are full-absorption averages, so avoiding one admission does not release the whole £3,690 — fixed overheads remain. The ~£920 midpoint is the cash the system actually banks.

Where it comes from

Earlier diagnosis, protected capacity, fewer readmissions.

01 — Upstream diagnosis

Flagging likely HFpEF patients in primary care means some are treated before reaching the front door.

02 — Protecting echo capacity

Raising pre-test probability sends scarce echocardiography to the patients most likely to actually have HFpEF.

03 — Reducing readmissions

22.3% of heart failure patients are readmitted within 30 days. Earlier guideline-directed therapy attacks that directly.

Modelled at a 10% upstream shift in currently-late diagnoses: roughly 6,000–7,600 admissions avoided per year, worth ~£4.5m–£8.4m. Modelled — not a forecast

Market

Built bottom-up, as a narrowing funnel.

The total patient population is not our market. The credible version narrows to the patients already inside a diagnostic pathway, with a budget attached and a bottleneck in front of them.

TAM · the whole condition

360–450k

People living with HFpEF in the UK, from a total heart failure prevalence of 920,616 cases. Around 190,000 new heart failure cases a year, of which HFpEF is 40–50%.

SAM · already being investigated

In-pathway

Patients presenting to primary care with suspected heart failure who are already receiving an NT-proBNP test and awaiting an echocardiogram. Already in a pathway, already funded, already queued.

SOM · measured in sites

Pilot ICBs

A defined number of Integrated Care Boards or Community Diagnostic Centres, multiplied by episodes per site per year. Sites, not population — that is the number a forecast can be built on.

Our beachhead: symptomatic suspected-heart-failure patients already waiting for an echocardiogram. Chosen because there is a defined clinical bottleneck, a defined budget holder, and an existing test to attach to. Broad population screening is a much larger market and a much worse first sale.
