Arterial stiffness screening for HFpEF

Half of all heart failures were invisible. Until now.

A passive 14-day wearable that finds the patients today's blood pressure cuff misses — before the emergency admission does.

The problem

She thought her blood pressure was normal. Three years later she had heart failure.

HFpEF is roughly half of all heart failure. Its pump looks fine on every standard test, so patients are reassured and sent away — until they arrive in crisis. The BP monitor that clears them was calibrated on one body type, and misreads everyone else.

~80%

of patients are diagnosed only after an emergency admission.

~50%

five-year mortality — comparable to many cancers.

1.7×

more likely to meet the diagnostic threshold if you're white, versus Black.

The cure exists. The patients exist. What doesn't exist is a way to find them.

The insight

The warning isn't weight or blood pressure. It's arterial stiffness.

As arteries stiffen with vascular ageing, the heart's ability to fill quietly declines — years before fluid, symptoms, or an admission make it visible to anyone else.

Our window
Days later
Today's tools catch it here
Too late

Published, not hypothetical: a 27-study, 6,626-patient meta-analysis found pulse wave velocity correlates with the echocardiographic markers used to diagnose HFpEF — and SGLT2 inhibitors now cut hospitalisation by ~26% once patients are found.

The solution

A wearable that watches your arteries — not just your pulse.

A chest hub and two optical sensors — wrist and earlobe — sharing one hardware clock, timing the pulse between the two sites to sub-millisecond precision. No cuff, no needle, no clinic visit.

Market & model

The cost of finding heart failure too late.

Heart failure costs the NHS ~£2bn a year, mostly paid late — at the most expensive possible point of detection. We're priced at what the system already pays for comparable diagnostics, sized bottom-up from patients already inside the diagnostic pathway today.
