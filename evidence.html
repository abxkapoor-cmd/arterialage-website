The insight

The warning isn't weight or blood pressure. It's arterial stiffness.

HFpEF is increasingly understood as a disease of the vasculature, not only the heart muscle. As the large arteries stiffen with vascular ageing, the workload on the heart rises, ventricular–arterial coupling deteriorates, and the heart's ability to fill declines.

Stage 01

Arterial stiffness rises

Measurable optically, passively, at home. Nothing in the current pathway looks here.

Stage 02

Fluid and weight rise

The first physical consequence. Detectable by a scale — if the patient steps on one.

Stage 03

Symptoms appear

Breathlessness, fatigue. Often dismissed as ageing or deconditioning.

Stage 04

Emergency admission

The event everyone was trying to prevent — and the point at which 80% of patients are first diagnosed.

The evidence

Published, not hypothetical.

Correlates with HFpEF

27 studies, 6,626 patients

A meta-analysis found pulse wave velocity correlates significantly with E/e′ and E/A — the echocardiographic markers used to diagnose HFpEF. Higher aortic PWV is associated with increased hospitalisation and cardiovascular mortality.

A treatable disease, now

SGLT2 inhibitors changed the case for early detection

HFpEF had no disease-modifying treatment until recently. Across DELIVER and EMPEROR-Preserved (12,251 participants), SGLT2 inhibitors cut first heart failure hospitalisation by a hazard ratio of 0.74 — roughly 26% — and are now guideline-recommended.

Pre-HFpEF

An earlier phenotype to catch

Pulse wave velocity may identify at-risk intermediate phenotypes before overt disease appears — a screening population no existing tool addresses.
