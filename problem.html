← Overview·01 — The problem

The stakes

She thought her blood pressure was normal. Three years later she had heart failure.

Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) accounts for roughly half of all heart failure, but diagnosis can take years. The standard tests look for a weak pump. A HFpEF patient's pump looks fine, so they are often reassured and sent away — until they arrive at A&E in crisis.

360–450k

people in the UK live with HFpEF, with 76,000–95,000 new cases each year.

~80%

of heart failure patients receive their initial diagnosis only after an emergency admission.

~50%

five-year mortality — comparable to many cancers.

~26%

reduction in heart failure hospitalisation from SGLT2 inhibitors — proven, but only in diagnosed patients.

The cure exists. The patients exist. What doesn't exist is a way to find them.

The warnings were there. The treatment was there. We just couldn't see it.

The root cause

Medicine was built for one kind of body. Yours may not be it.

Standard oscillometric BP monitors — used in virtually every GP surgery in the UK — were calibrated against a single baseline demographic: white males in their 60s. In patients with different arterial geometries and stiffness, these devices systematically misread. A patient can appear to have a normal blood pressure while their arteries tell a completely different story.

A pathway designed for the few

Echocardiography misses HFpEF by design

It screens for reduced ejection fraction. And it is the backlogged bottleneck: patients wait an average of 27 days between their first echocardiogram and their first heart failure admission.

The gold standard is out of reach

Invasive exercise cardiac catheterisation, available at fewer than ten designated UK centres.

The main symptom doesn't trigger referral

Vague breathlessness rarely escalates. GPs have no tool to separate the "worried well" from the quietly dying.

No home-based arterial monitoring exists

Nothing in any standard care pathway measures arterial stiffness ambulantly.

Who the calibration fails

Calibrated for

White males, age 60+.

Fails

Women · South Asian · Black · Elderly.

1.7×

White adults are 1.7 times more likely to meet the diagnostic threshold for HFpEF than Black adults.

Structural bias, baked into the most-used diagnostic tool in primary care — failing exactly the patients most at risk. Vascora Health closes this gap.
