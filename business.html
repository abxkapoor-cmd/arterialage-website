Differentiation

Everyone else measures one thing, once. We measure two, continuously.

Our price is validated by what the NHS already buys. The Zio XT is structurally the same product shape as ours — a 14-day, one-patient-at-a-time, AI-analysed cardiac diagnostic sold as a service — and the NHS already pays £229–265 for it. We are priced at the established rate for this category, not above it.

Defensibility

What is hard to copy.

Architecture

Two synchronised sites

Pulse transit time requires two sites sharing a hardware clock. A single-site patch cannot produce it.

Model

Six-feature risk classifier

Combining six features into a single HFpEF risk output is the core IP, and the reason we can hold a Zio-level price rather than a commodity-sensor price.

Evidence

Clinical dataset and Class IIa barrier

Consumer wearables do single-site rhythm detection, not two-site pulse wave velocity, and are not regulated for HFpEF screening.

Business model

The device is the razor. Each 14-day screen is the blade.

Pay-per-episode: we supply the device, the customer pays roughly £250 per 14-day patient screen. Because each device is cleaned and redeployed to the next patient, one unit earns revenue repeatedly rather than once.

Unit economics

A prototype costs ~£300; one episode repays it

Even at a conservative 10 uses a year, one device returns over £2,000 of annual contribution at an estimated ~85% margin. Working assumption

Why the NHS prefers this

Operational spend, not capital

Per-episode fees are opex, not capex — faster budget approval in a chronically capital-constrained system.

Why it compounds

No new logo needed to grow

Each device is cleaned and redeployed to the next patient, so revenue grows with usage, not with new sales.

Route to market

Regulatory clearance is not reimbursement. We do not wait for the second one.

Products with full regulatory approval, positive clinical data and even NICE guidance can spend years largely unused in the NHS, because the system has no mechanism to pay for them. Our route starts earning before national reimbursement exists.

Stage 1

Private clinics

Self-pay and private cardiology require only a UKCA mark. First revenue, first real-world data, no tariff dependency.

Stage 2

Local NHS pilots

Sell to an ICB or Community Diagnostic Centre from existing local budgets. This stage exists to generate the outcome data Stage 3 requires.

Stage 3

National funding routes

The MedTech Funding Mandate covers NICE-approved technologies that are cost-saving within three years and under £20m budget impact — our modelled £4.5–8.4m sits comfortably below that cap.

The buyer

The ICB commissioner

Commissioners hold the budget that pays for emergency admissions, so they bank the saving when admissions are avoided. The incentive aligns exactly.

Regulatory

Class IIa, UKCA — a known path.

Pulse-oximeter class (ISO 80601-2-61). MHRA registration plus conformity assessment by a UK Approved Body — typically 6–10 months from a complete submission. The real cost is the ISO 13485 quality system, not the registration fee.

Stage, stated plainly

What we have proved, and what we have not.

Proved — the physiology

Arterial stiffness is an early vascular signal of the same disease process, correlated with the echo markers used to diagnose HFpEF.

Open — sub-millisecond two-site timing

Our first validation milestone. Not novel physics — the risk is execution, not invention.

Design-mitigated — skin tone

Three wavelengths mitigate melanin effects; Monk-scale cross-skin-tone validation is mandated in our own specification.
